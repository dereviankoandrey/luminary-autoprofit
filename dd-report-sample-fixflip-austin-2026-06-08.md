## 3. Risk Assessment

### Red Flags ⚠️
1. **Holding costs destroy the deal:** At standard amortizing bridge terms, this flip loses money. The deal only works with interest-only financing AND fast execution. This is an unusual constraint for fix-and-flip — most deals absorb carrying costs better because of higher profit margins.
2. **ARV confidence needed:** $415K ARV assumes no market correction over 6-8 months. In a rising-rate environment, buyer purchasing power decreases, making ARV targets harder to achieve. Verify with at least 3 active buyers, not just comparable sales.
3. **Tight margin of safety:** Even in the best case (interest-only + 5-month close), the profit is only ~$22K on $285K acquisition — a 7.7% return for an 8-week active project. This doesn't justify the risk if anything goes wrong.

### Green Flags ✅
1. Purchase price/ARV ratio (68.7%) is excellent — the underlying asset economics are sound.
2. $125K renovation budget ($439/sqft assumed ~285 sqft reno/unit) suggests comprehensive interior work without being excessive.
3. Highway 360 corridor benefits from new development and infrastructure investment, supporting ARV confidence.

### Critical Unknowns
1. **ARV verification:** Has a recent comparable sale within 0.25 miles confirmed the $415K ARV? Without this, the entire analysis rests on an assumption.
2. **Permitting timeline:** Can renovations start immediately, or is there a permitting wait? Even 4 weeks of delay erodes profit by ~$7K in carrying costs.
3. **Contractor reliability:** Can a crew deliver quality work in 5-6 months consistently? This is the single biggest execution risk.
