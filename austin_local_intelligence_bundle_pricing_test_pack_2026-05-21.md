# Austin Local Intelligence Bundle — Pricing Test Pack

**Status:** buyer-validation asset  
**Created:** 2026-05-21 02:00 UTC cycle

## Purpose
Turn the bundled Austin offer into a cleaner pricing test than the brief-only version.

This pack is meant to answer:
1. whether buyers react better to the **bundle** than the **brief alone**
2. whether the permit-velocity layer increases paid interest
3. which price frame creates the least friction for the first warm tests

## Test goal
Compare two product framings:
- **Brief only:** Austin Entitlement Signal Brief
- **Bundle:** Austin Local Intelligence Bundle

Current pricing hypotheses:
- **Brief only:** $79/month solo, $199/month team
- **Bundle:** $99/month solo, $249/month team

Lead with:
- `austin_entitlement_signal_brief_sample_2026-05-19.md`

## Recommended 3-cell test

### Cell A — Bundle first
Use when the buyer likely feels both entitlement risk and permitting friction.

**Offer framing:**
- weekly Austin signal issue
- permit-velocity section
- operator implications
- watchlist
- official-source log

**Price:**
- $99/month solo
- $249/month team

### Cell B — Brief first, bundle upsell
Use when the buyer may prefer a simpler initial frame.

**Flow:**
1. offer the brief alone first
2. if relevant pain appears around timing or review drift, show the permit layer
3. ask whether the combined version is more compelling

### Cell C — A/B question without asking for payment first
Use when the relationship is warm but the buyer likely needs to react to the utility before price.

**Question:**
If you had to choose, which is more useful:
- a tight weekly entitlement brief
- or the same brief plus a permit-timeline and review-friction section

## What to learn from the first 5 tests
- Does the bundle get more curiosity than the brief-only frame?
- Does anyone say the permit section is the real reason they would pay?
- Does the bundle feel more complete or just more complicated?
- Is team pricing easier to defend than solo pricing?

## Success threshold
Useful early signal is any of:
- 1 buyer says the **bundle** is clearly more paid-useful than the brief alone
- 2 qualified buyers choose **bundle** when shown the comparison
- 1 buyer accepts pricing or asks to move forward

## Failure threshold
Revise positioning if:
- buyers consistently prefer the simpler brief-only framing
- the permit section creates confusion instead of stronger value
- pricing resistance rises without stronger relevance

## Why this matters now
The production workflow is no longer the main risk. The next bottleneck is discovering the narrowest framing that people will actually pay for. This pack is the shortest path to that answer without outreach automation, spend, or irreversible action.
