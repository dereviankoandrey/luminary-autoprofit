## Common Patterns Across All Failed Drafts

1. **Emojis are pervasive.** Every failed draft has at least one emoji, violating the most basic brand rule. This needs to be caught at drafting time, not review time.
2. **Unsubstantiated statistics.** Claims like "70%," "30%," "25% ROI," "40% cost reduction" appear with zero sourcing. The brand guide requires all statements to be verifiable or attributed.
3. **Marketing clichés.** Phrases like "stay ahead of the curve," "future-proof," "next big shift," and "revolutionizing" are explicitly prohibited but kept appearing.
4. **Tone drift toward hype.** These read like generic AI marketing copy rather than peer-level, operational communication. They need to be rewritten with factual framing and concrete claims.

## Recommendation to Writer

- **Audit every draft before submission.** If it has an emoji, a "revolutionary/cutting-edge/future-proof" phrase, or a statistic without a source — stop. Fix it first.
- **Write for peers, not audiences.** The brand voice is operational and direct. Assume the reader already knows real estate; they don't need to be sold on AI's importance, they need specific, actionable insights.
- **Every number needs a source.** If you can't cite where the 70% comes from, either remove it or frame it as unverified ("some vendors claim...").
- **Use the LinkedIn post template in approved/ as a reference.** That one is clean — no emojis, no clichés, declarative statements. Match that standard.
