At MLS pricing, Dallas flips are structurally unprofitable. The 2.8% property tax rate adds ~$7,840/year in carrying costs that compress already-tight margins.

Operator Intelligence: Off-market deals (probate, pre-foreclosure, direct-to-seller at 18–23% discount to asking) can work in Dallas if acquisition is $200K–$230K range. The flip margin lives entirely on the buy side, not the renovation or exit strategy.

Bottom line: Only viable for operators with established off-market sourcing channels. Not suitable for new flippers relying on MLS.

Dallas multifamily value-add is the strongest deal type in this market. Key drivers:

Employment anchors: Tesla Gigafactory (7,500 jobs), JPMorgan expansion (3,000+ jobs), and ongoing corporate relocations from California create structural rental demand that outpaces supply construction.

Rent gap: Post-renovation rents of $1,480/unit/month vs. current ~$1,315/unit/month represents a realistic 12% increase achievable through cosmetic upgrades without major structural work.

DSCR comfort margin: At 1.42x, there's meaningful cushion against rate volatility or vacancy spikes — well above the 1.20x threshold lenders typically require.

Risk factors: Texas property tax at 2.8% is highest in our validated pipeline. Construction labor costs are rising with population boom — renovation budget may need 5–8% contingency buffer.

Bottom line: Strong GO for experienced multifamily operators who can source off-market deals and execute renovations on time/budget.

For a pure investor buying the duplex and renting both units: negative cash flow at current interest rates. The 2.8% property tax rate is the primary driver.

However, for an owner-occupant (house-hack scenario), this converts to significantly positive:

  • Owner saves $1,400/month in imputed rent while paying down equity
  • Total net monthly benefit: approximately +710/mo when including imputed rent
Texas advantage: No-state-income-tax status makes the house-hack scenario more attractive than comparable deals in California or New York, where state income tax would further compress after-tax returns. The $25K–$30K annual state tax savings essentially subsidizes negative cash flow — making the house-hack math work better in Texas even with higher property taxes.

Bottom line: Viable as a path to homeownership for owner-occupants willing to accept modest monthly deficits. NOT recommended for pure investors at current rate environment.
