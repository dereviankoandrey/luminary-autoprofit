## Post Body

Two years ago I was sitting on a deal that "felt right" — good neighborhood, motivated seller, numbers looked fine on paper. I skipped three checkboxes on my due diligence list because they felt "boring." 

Six months later those three checkboxes were the reason I lost $47K. Not from market timing or bad luck. From skipping boxes labeled:
- HOA special assessments not disclosed by seller → unexpected $18K liability
- Unpermitted additions that failed inspection at sale → buyer backed out  
- Contractor estimates 40% below market → cut corners, $23K in rework

That deal taught me something most operators learn too late: gut feeling is just unstructured data. And unstructured data gives you confidence without accuracy (MIT Sloan study last week showed AI advice is "surprisingly good" but only when structured properly — same principle applies to deal analysis).

So I built the analytical framework I wish I had back then. Three templates, deterministic math, auditable decisions:

**1. Deal Scoring Matrix** — Score any deal across Profit Margin, Cost of Capital, Return on Cash, and Days on Market Sensitivity. Composite score with clear GO/NO-GO thresholds. No ambiguity.

**2. Financing Comparison Worksheet** — Compare every financing option side-by-side using total cost of capital, flexibility metrics, and risk-adjusted returns. Optimize your capital stack like a CFO.

**3. Due Diligence Checklist** — 6-phase verification framework with hard-blocker items that must be resolved before proceeding. Every item auditable. No skipped boxes.

This isn't another "how to invest in real estate" guide. It's the actual analytical framework I used across 20 years of development, underwriting, and $4B+ portfolio management — distilled into three templates anyone can use today.
