## Description

I lost $47K on a deal that "felt right" because I skipped three due diligence checkboxes. Those three boxes turned out to be undisclosed HOA assessments, unpermitted additions, and a contractor bid 40% below market rate.

That deal taught me that gut feeling is just unstructured data — and it gives you confidence without accuracy.

So I built the analytical framework from my 20 years of development work and underwriting across $4B+ in portfolios:

**Three templates, deterministic math, auditable decisions:**

1. **Deal Scoring Matrix** — Composite scoring system with GO/NO-GO thresholds across Profit Margin (30%), Cost of Capital (15%), Return on Cash (30%), and Days on Market Sensitivity (25%). Each category has A-F grades based on actual numbers, not feelings.

2. **Financing Comparison Worksheet** — Side-by-side capital stack optimization using total cost of capital, flexibility metrics, and risk-adjusted returns.

3. **Due Diligence Checklist** — 6-phase verification framework with hard-blocker items that must be resolved before proceeding. Title/legal, physical property, financials, market analysis, contractor validation, exit strategy.

The key insight: deterministic thresholds beat "it feels right" because you can audit every number. If the math says GO at ≥85 composite and NO-GO below 40, there's no room for self-justification on a bad deal.

Published on Gumroad ($47 one-time). Sample preview available above showing actual Phoenix deal analysis with full calculations.

Would love feedback from operators who've been through the gut-feel trap — what due diligence items did you learn to never skip?
