# Due Diligence Checklist — Operator-Grade Verification Framework

**Version:** 1.0 | **Updated:** 2026-07-29  
**Author:** Andrey Derevianko (20-year developer/CFO, $4B+ portfolio operator)  
**Philosophy:** Verify everything. Trust the documentation, not the seller's story. Every item on this list is a potential deal-killer — treat it that way.

---

## How to Use This Checklist

1. Print or use digitally — check off items as you complete them
2. Any unchecked item = unresolved risk. Document your decision to proceed anyway.
3. "NO-GO" items (marked with ⚠️) are hard blockers unless you have a verified mitigation plan
4. Save completed checklist with deal files — future-you will thank present-you when due diligence gaps surface

**Rule: If it's not documented, it doesn't exist.** Verbal assurances from sellers, agents, or contractors are entertainment. Not evidence.

---

## Phase 1: Title & Legal (Complete BEFORE offer)

### ⚠️ Hard Blockers — Deal cannot proceed unresolved

- [ ] **Preliminary title report ordered** (not just a county search — actual title company report)
- [ ] **No outstanding liens exceeding purchase price** — or seller has verified payoff plan
- [ ] **No pending litigation affecting the property** (mechanic's liens, boundary disputes, HOA suits)
- [ ] **Easements reviewed and understood** — utility, access, drainage, conservation
- [ ] **Zoning matches intended use** — verify with municipal planning department, not just Zillow

### Standard Verification

- [ ] **Property tax status confirmed** (current? delinquent? special assessments pending?)
- [ ] **Owner identity verified** — matching ID to title documents
- [ ] **HOA/COA review completed** (if applicable) — CCRs, bylaws, financial statements, meeting minutes
- [ ] **Survey obtained or ordered** — boundary verification, encroachment check
- [ ] **Permit history reviewed** — open permits = future liability

---

## Phase 2: Physical Property Inspection

### ⚠️ Hard Blockers

- [ ] **Structural inspection completed by licensed engineer/inspector** — no "looks fine" from general contractor
- [ ] **Roof age and condition verified** — remaining useful life documented (not estimated)
- [ ] **HVAC system age and condition documented** — replacement cost if <5 years remaining
- [ ] **Electrical panel type identified** — Federal Pacific, Zinsco, or other recalled panels = immediate $8K–$15K risk
- [ ] **Plumbing material identified** — polybutylene or galvanized pipes = replacement budget item

### Standard Verification

- [ ] **Foundation inspection** (slab/basement/crawlspace) — crack patterns documented with photos
- [ ] **HVAC age and remaining useful life** (typical: 15–20 years for HVAC, 20–30 for roof)
- [ ] **Water heater age** (tank: 8–12 years; tankless: 15–20 years)
- [ ] **Window condition and energy rating** — replacement cost per window × count
- [ ] **Exterior materials assessment** — siding, stucco, brick, trim condition
- [ ] **Drainage and grading** — water flows away from structure? Sump pump functional?
- [ ] **Pest/termite inspection** — active damage or previous treatment history

---

## Phase 3: Financial Verification (For Income Properties)

### ⚠️ Hard Blockers

- [ ] **Rent roll verified against actual leases** — not "seller says" numbers
- [ ] **Operating expenses audited against prior 24 months of statements/taxes** — no estimated expense projections from seller
- [ ] **Capital expenditure reserve established** — minimum 5% of effective gross income annually for multifamily

### Standard Verification

- [ ] **Vacancy rate analyzed** — market vacancy vs. actual vacancy documented separately
- [ ] **Tenant quality assessment** — credit scores, rental history, length of tenancy (where available)
- [ ] **Lease expiration schedule** — concentration risk if >30% of income from leases expiring in <12 months
- [ ] **Property management costs benchmarked** — 8–10% market rate for professional management
- [ ] **Insurance quotes obtained** — not seller's historical premiums (those are stale)

---

## Phase 4: Market & Location Analysis

### Standard Verification

- [ ] **Comparable sales reviewed** — last 90 days, same property type, ±25% size variance, within 1 mile
- [ ] **Absorption rate calculated** — how many similar properties sell per month in this submarket?
- [ ] **Neighborhood trajectory assessed** — new construction? Declining values? School district changes?
- [ ] **Planned developments researched** — municipal capital improvement plans, zoning change applications
- [ ] **Days on market trend analyzed** — increasing DOM = buyer's market pressure

---

## Phase 5: Contractor & Renovation Validation (For Fix-and-Flip/Value-Add)

### ⚠️ Hard Blockers

- [ ] **Detailed scope of work from licensed contractor** — not a verbal estimate or one-line quote
- [ ] **Scope includes line-item costs for every material and labor category**
- [ ] **Contractor license verified** with state licensing board
- [ ] **Contractor insurance (liability + workers' comp) confirmed current**

### Standard Verification

- [ ] **Second contractor bid obtained** — if bids vary by >15%, investigate why
- [ ] **Change order history reviewed** from contractor's previous projects (ask for references and call them)
- [ ] **Permit costs included in scope** — not an afterthought
- [ ] **Timeline includes buffer** — add 20% to contractor's estimated completion date
- [ ] **Payment schedule tied to inspection milestones** — never pay >10% upfront

---

## Phase 6: Exit Strategy Validation

### Standard Verification

- [ ] **Exit strategy documented in writing** (sale, refi+hold, sell+refi)
- [ ] **Exit timeline realistic** based on market absorption rates and renovation scope
- [ ] **Contingency exit plan defined** — if primary exit fails, what's the backup?
- [ ] **Refinance assumptions stress-tested** — LTV at refinance date vs. lender requirements

## Operator Notes

> "I've lost money on deals where I skipped one checkbox. Usually it was the 'boring' ones — easements, permit history, HOA financials. The exciting stuff (roof, foundation) you can see. The boring stuff is what silently drains value over time."
>
> — Andrey Derevianko

**Most common due diligence failures I've encountered:**
1. Unpermitted additions that fail inspection at sale → buyer backs out or demands credit
2. HOA special assessments not disclosed by seller → unexpected $5K–$30K liability
3. Contractor estimates 40% below market → either incompetent bid or will cut corners
4. "Seller financing available" with no written terms → never materializes into actual offer
5. Zoning doesn't allow intended use → discovered after closing, not before
