## Hard Money Lenders: Fast Cash, Expensive Cash

### How It Works
Hard money lenders lend based on the **property value and your exit strategy**, not your personal income or credit score. They're asset-based lenders — the collateral is the real estate, and their primary concern is whether they can sell it if you default.

### The Real Cost (Beyond the Interest Rate)

Most investors look at the interest rate and stop there. That's a mistake. Here's the complete cost breakdown for a typical hard money deal:

**Example: $250,000 loan on a fix-and-flip**

| Cost Component | Amount | Notes |
|---------------|--------|-------|
| Interest (11% annual × 6 months) | $8,250 | Based on 6-month hold assumption |
| Points (3 points upfront) | $7,500 | Paid at closing, reduces proceeds |
| Origination fee | $1,250 | Sometimes bundled with points |
| Appraisal fee | $400 | Required by lender |
| Flood cert | $20 | Mandatory if in flood zone |
| Inspection fee | $300 | Pre-renovation condition assessment |
| **Total Cost (6 months)** | **$17,720** | |
| **Effective Annual Rate** | **~14.8%** | True cost including all fees |

### When Hard Money Makes Sense
- You need to close in under 10 days (auction purchase, expired listing)
- The property has significant issues that disqualify it from conventional financing
- You're flipping and the hold period is short enough that total interest remains manageable
- You don't qualify for conventional loans due to self-employment income or credit issues

### When Hard Money Destroys Deals
- Hold periods extend beyond 9 months (interest compounds rapidly)
- The renovation takes longer than expected, eating into your profit margin
- Multiple draws with inspection delays (each delay = more interest payments)
- You underestimate rehab costs and need additional funding (hard money lenders often won't increase the loan mid-project)

## Private Money Lenders: Relationship-Based Capital

### How It Works
Private money comes from individuals — friends, family, fellow investors, or people you've built relationships with through networking. They lend their own capital, not institutional funds, which means terms are negotiable and relationship dynamics matter enormously.

**Real Austin example:** I ran a financing comparison on an actual property where hard money cost $34,290 in total interest over 18 months versus private money at $10,845 for the same loan amount and term. That's **$23,445 in savings** — pure profit retained from choosing the right capital source.

### Advantages of Private Money
- **Negotiable terms:** Interest rate, points, repayment schedule — everything is flexible
- **Lower costs:** Typically 2–4 percentage points below hard money rates
- **Patient capital:** Private lenders understand real estate cycles and are more willing to extend terms if a deal needs more time
- **No automated underwriting:** Decisions based on relationship + property value, not credit score algorithms

### Risks of Private Money
- **Relationship risk:** Defaulting on a family loan can destroy personal relationships
- **Limited capital availability:** Most private lenders have $50K–$200K to deploy, not millions
- **Inconsistent documentation:** Some private lenders skip formal paperwork (don't do this — always use legal agreements)
- **Opportunity cost for the lender:** They could earn 7–10% in S&P 500 index funds with zero effort

### How to Structure a Private Money Deal Professionally
```
Loan Amount: $200,000
Interest Rate: 8% annual (fixed)
Term: 18 months
Repayment: Interest-only monthly ($1,333/mo), principal due at maturity
Security: First lien position on the property
Default clause: 5% late fee after 10 days, acceleration clause after 60 days
Prepayment: No penalty (encourages early payoff if deal succeeds)
```

Always use a real estate attorney to draft the promissory note and security agreement. The $1,500 legal fee protects both parties enormously.

## Conventional DSCR Loans: Long-Term Wealth Building

### How It Works
DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) loans are conventional mortgages evaluated on the **property's income**, not your personal income. Lenders calculate whether the property generates enough rent to cover the mortgage payment, typically requiring a DSCR of 1.25 or higher:

```
DSCR = Net Operating Income ÷ Total Debt Service
Required minimum: Usually 1.25 (NOI must exceed debt obligations by 25%)
```

### The Numbers for Buy-and-Hold

**Example: $400,000 multifamily property with DSCR loan**

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Purchase Price | $400,000 |
| Down Payment (25%) | $100,000 |
| Loan Amount | $300,000 |
| Interest Rate | 7.5% (current market rate for DSCR) |
| Term | 30 years fixed |
| Monthly P&I | $2,098 |
| Monthly NOI (estimated) | $2,600 |
| **Monthly Cash Flow** | **$502** |
| **Annual Cash-on-Cash Return** | **~6.0%** |

### When Conventional DSCR Makes Sense
- Buy-and-hold or BRRRR strategy with long-term hold (7+ years)
- Property has strong, stable rental income already in place
- You want predictable payments and fixed-rate protection against rising rates
- Your goal is wealth accumulation through appreciation + principal paydown

### When Conventional DSCR Doesn't Work
- Fix-and-flip (hold period too short for conventional underwriting to make sense)
- Property needs significant renovation that affects occupancy during rehab
- You need speed (30–45 day closing is too slow for competitive markets)

## The Decision Framework: Which Financing Is Right For Your Deal?

Not every deal requires the same financing. Here's a structured approach to choosing:

### Step 1: Define Your Strategy
- **Fix-and-flip:** Short hold (3–9 months), value-add through renovation → Hard money or private money
- **BRRRR:** Buy, renovate, rent, refinance, repeat → Hard money initially, then DSCR refi
- **Buy-and-hold:** Long-term rental income → Conventional DSCR loan
- **Creative acquisition:** Seller financing or lease-option → Negotiated terms

### Step 2: Calculate Total Cost of Capital
Don't compare interest rates alone. Compare the **total cost over your expected hold period**:

```
Total Financing Cost = (Interest Rate × Hold Period) + Points/Fees + Any Additional Costs
```

A 12% hard money rate for a 6-month flip costs ~$7,500 in total financing on a $250K loan. A 7.5% DSCR loan held for 30 years costs approximately $455,000 in interest — but you're building equity and the property is cash-flowing during that entire period.

### Step 3: Factor in Flexibility
The cheapest financing isn't always the best if it locks you into rigid terms. Private money's flexibility (extendable terms, renegotiable rates) can save more money than a lower-rate hard money loan with strict deadlines and penalty structures.
