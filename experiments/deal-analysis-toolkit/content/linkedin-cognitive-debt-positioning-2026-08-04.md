## Positioning Statement (Full Post)

Most developers don't trust LLM output. They've learned to manually retype every line of code so they know exactly what it does before running it in production.

They call it "preventing cognitive debt." 

It's actually the same problem we face in real estate investing — but way worse because most decision-makers don't even have that level of scrutiny over their underwriting data.

When an AI can spend 2 hours and $10 writing code, generate screenshots at different points to verify it didn't mess up along the way... why would you trust an opaque model with a half-million-dollar investment decision?

We built deterministic underwriting tools for real estate because transparent math beats confidence any day. Not because we're anti-AI — because when money is on the line, auditable > impressive.

Real estate operators: are your deal analysis tools giving you numbers to trust or just pretty outputs that look right until they don't?

## Short-Form Version (if character limit issues)

Ankur Sethi's viral post yesterday hit on something most developers understand but rarely name: **"cognitive debt."** 

LLMs produce output. But operators learn fast — you can't trust code you can't trace back to its source. That's why thousands of devs now manually retype AI-generated functions just so they know what actually runs in production.

Same issue in real estate, but worse: most investors trust opaque deal analysis without ever questioning how the numbers were derived from raw data inputs. You wouldn't let an untrusted agent deploy production code. But we do exactly that with our biggest financial decisions daily. Deterministic underwriting = auditable math where any investor can see every step of the calculation. Not "AI said GO" — but "here's exactly why and how."
