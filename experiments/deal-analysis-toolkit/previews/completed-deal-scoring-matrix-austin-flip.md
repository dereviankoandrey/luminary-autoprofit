## Financial Metrics — Completed Analysis

### 1. Profit Margin (PM)

```
Holdings Costs = $13,800 (6mo interest/taxes/insurance estimated at $2,300/mo)
Disposition Costs = $685,000 × 7% = $47,950
Net Profit = ($685,000 - $425,000 - $95,000 - $13,800 - $47,950) = $103,250

PM = Net Profit / ARV × 100
PM = $103,250 / $685,000 × 100 = **15.1%**
```

**Score: C (12–17%)** — Marginal on paper. This is why price negotiation matters. At asking price, the margin eats into comfort zone. If we negotiate to $400K → PM jumps to 18.9% (B grade). The deal works with a better entry price.

---

### 2. Cost of Capital (COC)

```
Monthly COC = ($425,000 + $95,000 × 0.5) × (hard money rate ~12% annual / 12 monthly)
           = $472,500 × 1% per month = $4,725/month
Total Holding Costs ($13,800 estimate includes insurance/taxes, so COC portion = $4,725 × 6 = $28,350)

Annualized COC on total project cost:  
$28,350 / $472,500 × 12 months / 6 months of hold = **~12% annualized**
*(Note: This is hard money rate blended with the half-budget deferred draw)*
```

**Score: B (9–12%)** — Market rate for flip funding. Acceptable but not cheap. Hard money at 11-12% is standard for Texas fix-and-flip. If holding period extends, this becomes the primary profit killer.

---

### 3. Return on Cash (ROC)

```
Total Cash Invested = $50,000 (down payment ≈ 12%) + $8,000 (closing costs acquisition) + $95,000 (reno budget from line of credit/flex fund) - some reno drawn over time

Rough estimate: Total cash out the door at close = ~$58,000
Plus reno draw (funded through HELOC/purchase line): ~$80K of reno comes from hard money disbursements after rehab begins → not personal equity

Effective personal cash invested ≈ $58,000 + some contingency reserve = ~$70K net initial cash

ROC = Net Profit / Total Cash Invested × 100
On full project: ROC on $425K deal with ~$103K profit → depends on financing structure.

If all-cash (worst case for leverage): ROI = $103,250 / ($425,000 + $95,000) × 100 = **19.8%**
With hard money leverage: Cash-on-Cash ≈ **28.7%** (as calculated in Flash Analysis #7)

Score: B → A (25–34%, leans toward A with this leverage structure)
```

**Score: B (Strong)** — 28.7% cash-on-cash with typical hard money financing is a solid return for a 6-month hold period.

---

### 4. Days on Market Sensitivity (DMS)

```
Stress Test: If holding period extends from 6 to 9 months (+3 months):

Additional holding costs ≈ $2,300 × 3 = $6,900 (increased interest/insurance/taxes)  
If market softens and takes additional 3 months past the 35-day estimated resale → adds ~$14K in carrying costs

New Net Profit with +3mo delay: $103,250 - $13,800 = $89,450
Would still result in ~13% profit margin. Still positive but significantly compressed.

Score: Handles moderate delay well for a South Austin deal (fast absorption area).
```

**Score: B (Handles +4 month delay acceptably)** — South Austin's 21-35 day average DOM provides good exit floor even in softening markets.
