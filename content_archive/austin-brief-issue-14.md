## Executive Summary — The FY27 Budget Didn't Pass. Here's What Actually Happened:

The July 21-23 Pentathlon Week did NOT resolve the FY2026-2027 budget to final passage. Instead, it produced what analysts should understand as a *de facto FY27 framework* — significant appropriations, new bond election authority, major infrastructure commitments, and critical zoning/permitting changes that will shape Austin development for 3+ years forward.

**The signal is clear:** developers holding capital on the sidelines until "final passage" are overplaying procedural formality. The items approved during Pentathlon Week *are* operational now — they don't wait for a ceremonial final vote to take effect.

### Five Signals That Matter:

1. **$320M Battery Storage Agreement with Jupiter Power** (Item 86/87) — this is the largest new municipal infrastructure commitment in recent Austin history at $320 million over 20 years, at up to $16M/year. For developers with sites near industrial corridors or logistics parks: electrical capacity constraints that have been a deal-killer may ease significantly starting FY27.

2. **November 2026 Bond Election Authorized** (Item 4) — Council voted the framework for a bond election in November 2026, including recommended bond measures and proposition language for public improvements. For development: new transportation, park, trail, and water infrastructure funding potential that should be underwritten into pro formas immediately.

3. **FY27 Assessment Roll Adopted** (Items 101-103) — the official assessment roll and service plan for FY2026-2027 was approved with accompanying draft ordinances. This is THE document developers need to check before committing capital: which districts get new services, what TIF zones are impacted, how franchise fees shift across corridors.

4. **Special Events at Parks Regulatory Shift** (Item 2) — ordinance moving special events requirements from Chapter 8-1 to Chapter 4-20 and updating definitions for "new special events at certain parks." Developers with mixed-use or entertainment components near park districts should map the new coverage areas immediately.

5. **AHFC $3.95M Bolm Road Land Acquisition** — AHFC Board authorized acquisition of 6.041 acres at/near 4800 Bolm Road using 2022 GO bonds (FY25-26 Capital Budget). This is directly development-relevant: Travis County housing credit competition for sites south-east of central Austin, near transit corridors.

## Signal 2: November 2026 Bond Election Framework Voted — Public Improvement Funding Ahead [HIGH IMPACT]

**Source:** July 21 Work Session / July 23 Regular Meeting Item 4  
**Confirmed:** Resolution directs City Manager to prepare ordinance ordering a November 2026 bond election with "recommended bond measures, proposition language, categories, and amounts for public improvements"

**Why this means money in development pipelines:**

A bond election means Austin is raising capital through voter-approved debt. Historically, these bonds fund:
- Transportation (roads, transit extensions like Rail Park phases)
- Water infrastructure (treatment capacity, transmission main expansion into developing areas)  
- Parks/trails (Greenway System Phase 4 and beyond)
- Flood management (detention basins, watershed improvements)

"Categories and amounts" means the specific line items haven't been published yet — but the *existence* of a November election is itself actionable data: it tells developers that Austin plans to borrow hundreds of millions for public improvements in the 2026 calendar year. For projects currently stalled waiting on infrastructure availability, this timeline should be factored into underwriting immediately rather than treated as "pre-meeting speculation."

**Operator action:**
1. Map your active pipeline properties against historically bond-funded corridors — items frequently funded include arterial roads (Metric infrastructure), water transmission lines serving Travis County growth areas, and Greenway trail connections for multi-family density bonuses
2. Track the ordinance preparation — once City Manager files it, exact amounts become public and subscribers should act within 48 hours to adjust pro formas

These are THE documents every developer should be reviewing for FY2026-2027. The assessment roll defines:

- Service area boundaries that determine whether properties get sewer/water extension at city-subsidized rates versus full-cost developer impact fees

**Operator action:**

3. If a property sits in or near an expanded service district, the difference between municipal-subsidized infrastructure and private-pay could be $500K-$2M+

## Signal 4: AHFC Bolm Road Land Acquisition ($3.95M) — Housing Credit Competition Shift [MEDIUM-HIGH]

**Source:** July 23 AHFC Board Meeting Item 002  
**Confirmed:** Negotiation and execution authorized for acquisition of ~6 acres at/near 4800 Bolm Road  
**Funding Source:** $3.95M available from FY25-26 Capital Budget using 2022 General Obligation Bonds

**Why this matters:**

The Bolm Road corridor (SE Austin, south-east of downtown near the Airport) is an active development zone for multi-family and mixed-use projects competing for AHFC tax credit allocations. A government-accelerated land acquisition in this area changes:
- Competition density (potential new affordable housing supply from AHFC directly competes with private-market deals)  
- Land valuation baselines (government acquisition price often establishes a floor value that surrounding private transactions reference)
- Transit corridor priority (Bolm Road is a major CapMetro corridor — transit-oriented development incentives may follow)

**Operator action:** Track whether this 6-acre AHFC purchase triggers any rezoning or density changes to the surrounding zone, and model increased affordability competition into Travis County underwriting that goes forward.
