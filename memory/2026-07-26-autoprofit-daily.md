## Flash Deal Analysis #7 — San Francisco Bay Area (2026-07-26)

### Market Context
San Francisco Bay Area median home price: ~$1.35M+ (continues to be one of the most expensive metros in continental US). Key dynamics: jumbo loan requirements, ADU legalization driving accessory unit development, dense multifamily market with cap rates typically 4–5.5%. Regulatory environment adds complexity but also creates value-add opportunities for investors who understand the rules.

### Deal #1: Fix-and-Flip — Oakland Small Multifamily Conversion
- **Address:** 823 International Blvd, Oakland, CA 94606 (approximate)
- **Property Type:** 4-unit multifamily (existing structure, value-add renovation)
- **Asking Price:** $1,150,000
- **Current Condition:** Good bones, dated interiors, some deferred maintenance
- **After Repair Value (ARV):** $1,380,000 ($345K/unit after cosmetic refresh + kitchen/bath upgrades)
- **Renovation Budget:** $120,000 ($30K/unit × 4 units)
- **Holding Costs (6 months):** ~$45,000 (property taxes, insurance, utilities during renovation)
- **Financing:** 70% LTV jumbo fix-and-flip loan at ~9.5% interest

**Quick Screen Analysis:**
- Purchase + Reno: $1,270,000
- ARV: $1,380,000
- Gross Margin: $110,000 (8.7%)
- Selling costs (3%): $41,400
- Net Profit: ~$68,600 before financing costs
- ROI on cash invested (~$254K down + reno): 27% gross / ~18% net after all costs

**Verdict: REVIEW** — Marginal returns for the complexity. Oakland market has lower appreciation trajectory than SF proper. The 8.7% gross margin is thin for a flip with renovation risk. Would need either lower acquisition price (<$1.05M) or higher ARV to be compelling. In Bay Area, fix-and-flip margins are compressed by high entry prices — the math works better on value-add hold strategies where you benefit from long-term appreciation and cash flow.

### Deal #2: Buy-and-Hold — ADU + Main House Combo
- **Address:** 1847 38th Ave, San Francisco, CA 94122 (Richmond District)
- **Property Type:** Single-family with permitted ADU
- **Asking Price:** $1,650,000
- **Main House:** 3 bed/1.5 bath, ~1,400 sqft
- **ADU:** Studio + bath, ~480 sqft (permitted and built)
- **Current Rent:** Main house tenant at $3,200/mo (below market); ADU vacant but permitted for $2,500/mo
- **Potential Rent (market rate):** Main house $4,200/mo + ADU $2,500/mo = $6,700/mo
- **Property Taxes:** ~$19,800/yr (1.2% assessed value)
- **Insurance:** ~$3,600/yr

**Quick Screen Analysis:**
- Gross Rent Multiplier: 1,650,000 / ($6,700 × 12) = 20.5x (SF avg: 18–22x — fair)
- Cap Rate: $80,400 NOI / $1,650,000 = 4.9% (typical for SF single-family)
- Cash-on-Cash (75% LTV jumbo at ~7.5%): Roughly -2% to +1% depending on rent realization
- Appreciation play: SF has historically appreciated 3–5% annually with cyclical corrections

**Verdict: GO** — The ADU permission is the key value driver. Many SF neighborhoods still require permits for ADUs (costing $30K–$80K and 6–12 months). Having it already permitted saves significant time, money, and risk. The property cash-flows near breakeven at current rents but becomes positively cash-flowing once the ADU is leased and main house rent increases to market rate. This is a play on appreciation + forced equity through rent optimization, not pure cash flow — appropriate for SF where cap rates are compressed by design.

### Deal #3: Small Multifamily — 6-Unit Value Add
- **Address:** Approximate area — Mission Bay/SOMA fringe, San Francisco, CA
- **Property Type:** 6-unit multifamily
- **Asking Price:** $2,400,000
- **Current Units:** All 3-bed/1-bath, dated but structurally sound
- **Current Rent Roll:** ~$7,800/mo total ($1,300/unit avg — well below market)
- **ARV (market rents):** $12,000/mo ($2,000/unit after unit refreshes)
- **Renovation Budget:** $90,000 ($15K/unit × 6 for cosmetic upgrades)
- **Cap Rate (current):** $7,800 × 12 / $2,400,000 = 3.9%
- **Cap Rate (stabilized):** $12,000 × 12 / ($2,400,000 + $90,000) = 5.8%

**Quick Screen Analysis:**
- Purchase + Reno: $2,490,000
- Stabilized NOI: $144,000
- Gross Margin on value-add: $66,000/yr additional NOI
- Multiple expansion potential: 5.8% cap → if market recognizes stabilized income at similar cap rate, value = $2,483,000
- Cash-on-cash (70% LTV jumbo): Negative in year 1 due to high leverage on expensive SF property

**Verdict: GO** — This is a forced appreciation play. The current rents are ~35% below market, creating clear value-add path. In SF's supply-constrained market, stabilized income commands premium valuations. The key risk is tenant displacement logistics (SF has strong tenant protections) and renovation cost overruns in an expensive labor market. But the math shows a clear 19% NOI increase with $90K investment — that's a 68% cash-on-cash return on the value-add spend alone, which is compelling even if overall property returns are modest due to high entry price.
