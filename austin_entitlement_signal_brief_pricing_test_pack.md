## Recommended 3-cell test

### Cell A — Direct paid pilot
Best for buyers already close to the pain.

**Message goal:** ask for paid interest directly.

**Offer:**
- weekly issue
- 5-8 source-backed signals
- operator implications
- watchlist + source log
- $79 solo / $199 team

**Best for:**
- developers
- acquisitions leads
- lenders or debt brokers already monitoring Austin

### Cell B — Sample-first then price
Best for warmer but less urgent buyers.

**Message goal:** get them to review a sample before presenting price.

**Flow:**
1. ask permission to send sample
2. send sample
3. ask if this solves a current monitoring problem
4. then offer $79 / $199 pilot

**Best for:**
- consultants
- capital partners
- contacts who know Austin but may not own the workflow directly

### Cell C — Founding-member framing
Best for buyers who may respond to scarcity or early-access framing.

**Message goal:** test whether founding-member language increases response quality.

**Offer framing:**
First 10 pilot subscribers keep current pricing for 6 months and can shape the first 4-week issue mix.

## 5-question response script
Use only after interest is shown.

1. Are you actively exposed to Austin development, lending, acquisitions, or entitlement-related workflow right now?
2. How do you currently monitor city/process changes that can affect timelines or underwriting?
3. Is this pain mostly for one person or a team?
4. If this worked well, would solo pricing or team pricing fit better?
5. What would make this clearly worth paying for: time saved, better underwriting awareness, internal reporting convenience, or something else?

## Success threshold
A useful early pass is any of:
- 1 buyer agrees to pay
- 2 buyers say current pricing is reasonable and ask for follow-up
- 3 qualified conversations confirm this solves an existing workflow pain

## Failure threshold
The current pricing/positioning likely needs revision if after 10 warm tests:
- nobody wants a sample
- sample interest does not convert into workflow relevance
- repeated feedback says the brief is useful but not paid-useful
- repeated feedback says the offer needs broader market scope

## Recommended testing sequence
1. start with 3 warmest likely-fit names in Cell B
2. send 2 stronger-conviction names through Cell A
3. use Cell C only if response volume is weak but relevance seems real
4. after 5 total tests, compare objections before changing price

## Decision rule after first 5 tests
- **Keep price** if at least 2 reactions are neutral-to-positive
- **Adjust offer framing** if relevance is clear but purchase intent is weak
- **Broaden scope carefully** only if multiple qualified buyers want a wider market-intel product instead of a narrow signal brief
