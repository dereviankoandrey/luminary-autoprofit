# Austin Market Intel Subscription — Validation Spec v1

## Purpose
Define the smallest recurring version of an Austin market-intel product that can be produced with high agent autonomy and minimal capital.

## Core Offer
A weekly or biweekly subscription for developers, investors, lenders, and brokers focused on Austin-area signals that affect acquisitions and capital decisions.

## Candidate Deliverables
### Option A — Weekly Signal Brief
- permit and zoning highlights
- major financing / sales / development announcements
- rent / supply / demand snapshots from public or low-cost sources
- short "what changed this week" commentary

### Option B — Submarket Watchlist
- 3-5 target submarkets
- notable pipeline changes
- comparable project movement
- risk / opportunity flags

### Option C — Deal-Ops Trigger Feed
- concise alerts for new information likely to matter for sourcing or underwriting
- meant for operators screening opportunities, not general-news readers

## ICP
- small developers
- acquisition teams
- capital allocators
- debt brokers / lenders
- consultants who need a repeatable local signal digest

## Recurring Revenue Shape
- solo subscription: $99-199/month
- team subscription: $299-749/month
- premium custom watchlist: higher-touch add-on only after repeatable base product exists

## Why This Could Work
- local information asymmetry can matter more than generic national news
- recurring format is possible if production is templated
- agent can collect, normalize, summarize, and format much of the brief
- can be validated with one sample issue before heavier buildout

## Main Risks
- may drift into bespoke research
- public-data collection may be inconsistent
- differentiation may be weak without a sharp thesis and repeatable format
- acquisition likely depends on warm distribution unless the content is unusually strong

## Minimum Autonomous Workflow
1. collect raw source updates from defined public sources
2. deduplicate and classify by topic / submarket
3. score each item for likely relevance to investors / developers
4. generate concise summaries and implications
5. assemble into one fixed-format issue
6. review for hallucination / source accuracy

## Validation Standard
This idea earns promotion only if a sample issue can be assembled quickly, feels decision-useful, and avoids turning into custom analyst work.

## Smallest Next Test
Build one sample issue with a fixed template and source list, then judge:
- is the output differentiated?
- is it repeatable weekly with light human QA?
- would a specific local operator plausibly pay for it?
