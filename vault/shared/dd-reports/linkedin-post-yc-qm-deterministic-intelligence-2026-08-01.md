## Post Text

YC just open-sourced QM. 520 points on Hacker News in hours.

It's a "multiplayer agent harness" — essentially infrastructure that lets every employee at a startup have their own isolated AI workspace while collaborating across Slack and shared projects. Each person gets scoped memory, independent sandboxes, configurable security postures (strict approval gates to fully automatic), and the ability to plug in different AI backends.

I've been building deterministic underwriting tools for real estate investors for months now. Here's what QM tells me about where this is heading:

**1. Multi-agent is becoming the default model.**  
The framing isn't "AI assistant" anymore — it's "multiplayer." YC isn't selling a tool; they're defining how startups work with AI going forward. Every startup gets their own agent workforce. This validates what I've been telling operators: AI that works alone in a chat window is the wrong model. Agents need to be coworkers with boundaries, permissions, and accountability.

**2. The security posture spectrum IS our product category.**  
QM offers three modes: Strict (every tool call pauses for human approval), Auto (classifier screening), Dangerous (no gates). This isn't just a configuration menu — it's a trust framework. When an AI touches financial decisions, operators don't want "smart." They want auditable. Every calculation traceable. Every decision explainable to their investors or partners.

**3. Infrastructure vs intelligence.**  
QM is the plumbing. It doesn't solve real estate underwriting, due diligence, or deal analysis. That's where domain-specific intelligence lives — and that's what operators actually pay for. The infrastructure layer will be commoditized. The vertical expertise won't.

I've spent 20 years as a CFO and developer across $4B+ in real estate development. I built an underwriting engine where every calculation is deterministic and auditable because probabilistic AI making financial decisions is a liability, not an asset.

The market is converging on this: verifiable intelligence > black-box intelligence. Not because deterministic tools are smarter — because they're honest about what they know and don't know.

QM by YC confirms the infrastructure thesis. The opportunity for operators who build domain-specific AI skills inside those platforms? That's where real value gets created.
