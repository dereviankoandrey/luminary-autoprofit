## Market Overview — Houston

Houston is the 4th largest metro in the US (~7.3M population) with fundamentals that make it uniquely attractive for buy-and-hold investors:

- **Median home price:** ~$310K (fix-and-flip ACOTarget range $260K–$420K depending on submarket)
- **No state income tax** — directly boosts cash flow vs. Nashville/DC
- **Diverse economy:** Energy, healthcare (Texas Medical Center = largest in world), aerospace, port/logistics
- **Population growth:** +150K net migration/year (consistent, not speculative)
- **Inventory:** ~3.8 months supply (balanced to slightly seller-favorable in established suburbs)
- **Days on market:** 32–45 days depending on price tier and submarket
- **Property tax rate:** Effective ~1.8% (high but offset by no state income tax; homestead exemptions reduce effective burden for owner-occupants)
- **Insurance risk:** Moderate — flood zones require separate coverage ($1,500–$3,500/yr in high-risk areas), but not as extreme as Florida

**Key differentiator from other validated markets:** Houston has the deepest buyer pool among Sun Belt metros (4th largest metro = 2.5x population of Nashville). This creates superior exit liquidity for both flips and rentals. The tradeoff is higher property taxes, which we factor into underwriting.

## Deal Analysis

### Deal Type 1: Fix-and-Flip — Midtown Houston (Near Downtown)

**Parameters:**
- Purchase price (off-market probate): $285K
- ARV (after renovation): $430K
- Renovation budget: $75K
- Holding costs (6 months @ 9.5% interest): ~$18K
- Closing costs (buy + sell, ~3.5% total): ~$23K
- Total investment: $381K + holding = **~$419K**

**Exit Analysis:**
- ARV: $430K
- Estimated selling costs (6% agent + transfer): ~$25.8K
- Net sale proceeds: $404.2K
- Profit: $404.2K - $419K = **-$14.8K**

**Verdict: NO-GO at MLS pricing**  
Houston's near-downtown flip margins are thin because land values have appreciated faster than renovation costs create value. Off-market deals (probate/pre-foreclosure) at 15–20% below market could work, but the channel-dependent flip pattern seen in Phoenix and DC-Arlington holds here too.

**Operator note:** Houston's size means submarket variation is extreme. Baytown/Pasadena flips may have different economics than Midtown. This analysis applies to established inner-loop neighborhoods only.

### Deal Type 2: Multifamily 10-Unit Value-Add — Clear Lake / Seabrook Suburb

**Parameters:**
- Purchase price (value-add opportunity): $2,850K ($285K/unit)
- Current NOI: $195K (6.8% cap rate — below market for submarket)
- Renovation budget per unit: $4K × 10 = $40K
- Rent uplift post-renovation: +$35/unit/month → +$42K/year NOI
- New NOI: $237K
- ARV Cap Rate (conservative): 7.5%
- New property value at ARV cap rate: $3,160K

**Financing:**
- Loan-to-value: 75% → $2,138K loan @ 9.0%, 30yr amort
- DSCR (current): $195K / ~$204K annual debt = **0.96x** (below 1.0 — needs value-add to qualify)
- DSCR (post-renovation): $237K / ~$204K = **1.16x**

**Profit Analysis:**
- Equity required: $2,850K - $2,138K + $40K Reno + $30K closing/working capital = **~$742K**
- Value creation: $3,160K - $2,850K - $40K = $270K equity gain
- Cash-on-cash (Year 1): ($237K - $204K debt service) / $742K = **4.5%**

**Verdict: REVIEW — Requires SBA or creative financing to bridge DSCR gap**  
The deal works on a hold-and-appreciate basis but fails traditional bank underwriting at current rates. Houston's strong rental demand (population growth + no state tax) means the risk is lower than similar deals in slower markets, but the math requires either:
- SBA 504 loan (better DSCR requirements, lower rate ~7%)
- Seller financing bridge for initial acquisition
- Joint venture with equity partner

**Not a standalone cash-flow play at current rates.** But Houston's structural demand floor makes this a strong appreciation play if the buyer can bridge the initial DSCR gap.

### Deal Type 3: Buy-and-Hold Duplex — Katy Suburb (West Houston)

**Parameters:**
- Purchase price: $340K
- Unit A rent (market): $1,650/month
- Unit B rent (market): $1,550/month
- Total monthly income: $3,200
- Operating expenses (35% of income): $1,120
- Property tax (~1.8% effective with homestead): ~$495/month
- Insurance: ~$180/month
- Maintenance reserve: ~$160/month
- Total monthly expenses (excl. debt): ~$1,955

**Financing:**
- Loan @ 75% LTV: $255K @ 9.0%, 30yr
- Monthly P&I payment: ~$2,056

**Monthly Cash Flow:**
- Income: $3,200
- Total expenses (incl. debt): $4,011
- **Net monthly deficit: -$811/month**

**House-hack scenario (owner occupies one unit):**
- Owner saves $1,550/month rent
- Actual net cost: $811 - $1,550 = **+$739/month positive cash flow**
- Plus principal paydown: ~$246/month → total benefit: +$985/month

**Verdict: REVIEW — Strong house-hack signal only**  
At current interest rates (9%+), duplexes in Houston are negative cash flow for pure investors but excellent for owner-occupants. Katy's strong school district and suburban amenities create deep rental demand, ensuring low vacancy risk when the owner eventually moves out.

**Operator note:** This is a "live free now, rent later" strategy. The ~$800/month deficit is essentially a rent-reduction investment that converts to positive cash flow once the owner moves into one unit. Houston's no-state-income-tax status makes this more attractive than comparable deals in Nashville or DC.

## Houston-Specific Operator Intelligence

1. **Channel-dependent flip economics confirmed again.** Markets with rapid land appreciation (Phoenix, DC-Arlington, Houston near-downtown) require off-market deals for profitable flips. MLS pricing is structurally unprofitable in these submarkets.

2. **Houston's size creates submarket variation that generic market reports miss.** A deal that fails in Midtown could easily pass in Katy or Cypress. The agent pipeline would need to support submarket-level analysis for Houston specifically — this is a product enhancement opportunity for the underwriting engine (add ZIP-code-level customization).

3. **No state income tax + diverse economy = structural demand floor.** Unlike Phoenix (energy-dependent) or Nashville (tourism-sensitive), Houston's economy spans energy, healthcare, aerospace, and logistics. This creates more resilient rental demand across economic cycles.

4. **Insurance risk is manageable but must be underwritten separately.** Flood zone properties require additional coverage ($1.5K–$3.5K/yr). The engine should flag flood-zone properties as a separate line item in expense analysis.
