**Analysis:** Chicago flips are structurally unprofitable at MLS pricing. Two compounding factors: (1) the 65-day average DOM means holding costs run ~$1,825/month vs $937/month in Dallas — nearly double carrying cost per day, and (2) the 2.1% property tax adds ~$4,200/year that doesn't exist in Sun Belt markets with sub-1% effective rates.

This extends the channel-dependent flip pattern confirmed across Houston, Phoenix, and Dallas. Chicago confirms it's not a Sun Belt phenomenon — **MLS flips are structurally unprofitable across all tested US markets** when acquisition is at market price.

**Operator intelligence:** Off-market deals in Chicago work better than in Sun Belt markets because entry prices are lower ($150K-$200K for solid 3-bed/2-bath homes in neighborhoods like Pilsen, Bridgeport, or Englewood). The flip margin lives entirely on the buy side. Operators with established off-market sourcing (direct-to-seller at 30-40% discount to MLS) can achieve ARV margins of $40K-$60K — but this requires serious sourcing infrastructure.

**Bottom line:** Only viable for operators with proven off-market channels and local contractor relationships. Not suitable for new flippers or those relying on MLS/Zillow/Redfin. The low entry price is deceptive — carrying costs erase the advantage.

**Analysis:** Chicago multifamily value-add is the strongest deal type in this market — and potentially the strongest across all validated markets. Key drivers:

1. **Entry price advantage:** At $1.4M for 8 units ($175K/unit), entry prices are 30-40% below comparable Sun Belt deals (Dallas at ~$308K/unit, Miami at ~$230K/unit). This creates a margin of safety that Sun Belt markets lack.
2. **Rent uplift potential:** Chicago rents have been suppressed relative to national trends for years. Unit renovations (new kitchens, bathrooms, flooring) can capture 15% rent increases because the gap between "dated" and "modern" units is larger here than in markets where every unit was recently renovated.
3. **Employment stability:** Unlike Sun Belt markets dependent on single-company expansions, Chicago's economy is diversified across finance (CME Group: 5,000+ employees), tech (Google Chicago: 2,000+ employees and growing), healthcare (Rush University Medical Center: 20,000+ employees), and government. This creates a rental demand floor that survives economic downturns better than Sun Belt markets.
4. **DSCR comfort:** At 1.36x, there's meaningful cushion against rate volatility — above the 1.20x threshold lenders require.

**Risk factors:**
- Illinois property tax at 2.1% is the highest in our pipeline (vs Dallas 2.8%, but Chicago-area rates are more uniform and consistently high across all municipalities)
- Winter maintenance costs add ~$96,000/year across 8 units (~$1,000/unit/year for snow removal, roof maintenance, HVAC cycling) — factored into NOI projections conservatively
- Illinois has tenant-protection laws stronger than most states (security deposit limits, eviction moratoriums during extreme weather). Landlord-friendly operations require local legal awareness.

**Bottom line:** This is the strongest deal type across all 14 validated markets. The combination of low entry prices, high rent uplift potential, and diversified employment creates a compelling risk/reward profile. Best suited for operators who can source off-market deals below $1.2M (cap rate compression opportunity) and execute renovations efficiently.

## Deal #3: Duplex Buy-and-Hold (House Hack)

**Scenario:** $260K purchase → owner occupies one unit, rents other  
**Verdict:** ❌ **NO-GO (even as house-hack)**

| Insurance + maintenance reserve | ~$260 |
| Total monthly expense | ~$2,205 |
| Rental income (tenant unit) | ~$1,350 |
| Net cash flow (pure investor) | **-$873/month** ❌❌ |
| Imputed rent benefit (owner lives in one unit) | +$1,100/month |
| Net economic benefit (house-hack scenario) | **+$227/month** ⚠️ marginal |

**Analysis:** Chicago duplex buy-and-hold is the weakest deal type across all 14 validated markets. Two structural factors make it unviable:

1. **Property tax drag at 2.1%:** Adds ~$5,460/year in carrying costs — significantly higher than Dallas ($9,520/year on a $340K purchase = 2.8%, but the Chicago duplex is cheaper so absolute amount is lower, yet the rate still compresses cash flow more aggressively)
2. **Lower rental income relative to purchase price:** Chicago rents are moderate (~$1,350/unit for a second unit in a duplex vs $1,650 in Dallas). The rent-to-price ratio is unfavorable — you're paying $260K for a property where one unit only generates $1,350/month.

Even as a house-hack (owner-occupant scenario), the net benefit of +$227/month is marginal and doesn't justify the risk/reward. Compare this to Dallas's +$710/month house-hack benefit or Miami's stronger buy-and-hold numbers in prior validated markets.

**State income tax consideration:** The 4.95% Illinois state income tax further compresses after-tax returns for out-of-state investors. While depreciation can offset some of this, the net effect is still negative compared to no-state-tax jurisdictions.

**Bottom line:** NOT recommended for any investor type — neither pure cash-flow seekers nor owner-occupants. Chicago's combination of high property taxes and moderate rents creates a deal structure where the math simply doesn't work at duplex scale. If pursuing buy-and-hold in Chicago, target 4+ unit properties (see Deal #2 above) where economies of scale and rent uplift potential overcome the tax drag.
