## Why This Matters

Generic outreach from a warm contact gets 15-20% response rate. Personalized outreach from a warm contact can hit 40-60%. The difference isn't the product — it's making the recipient feel like you thought about *their* business before reaching out.

Since Andrey knows these people personally, even one specific detail transforms an email from "another pitch" to "a peer who gets me."

## The 30-Second Personalization Framework

Before sending any outreach email, answer these three questions about the prospect:

### 1. What do they actually do? (Deal Type + Market)
**Where to find it:** LinkedIn "About" section, fund website, recent closing announcements, local CRE forum posts  
**Extract:** Their primary deal type (value-add multifamily, fix-and-flip, development) and active market(s)

**Examples:**
- Marcus Chen — value-add multifamily in Nashville & Charlotte
- Sarah Torres — BRRRR operator, single-market in Houston
- James O'Brien — ground-up development, East Austin corridor

### 2. What's their scale? (Portfolio Size / Deal Velocity)
**Where to find it:** LinkedIn experience (years at current firm), fund AUM if disclosed, recent deal volume mentions  
**Extract:** Solo operator → small syndicator ($5-30M AUM) → large fund ($100M+)

**Why it matters:** 
- Solo operators care about **time savings** ("I'm spending 8 hours a week underwriting")
- Small syndicators care about **deal flow capacity** ("we're limited by how many deals we can evaluate")
- Large funds care about **consistency & standardization** ("our analysts produce uneven quality")

### 3. What's their recent activity? (The Hook)
**Where to find it:** LinkedIn recent posts, property listing announcements, county assessor records for active acquisitions, local CRE news  
**Extract:** A specific deal they recently bought/sold/listed, or a market topic they've commented on

**Examples:**
- "Saw your closing on the 24-unit in Berry Hill — congrats"
- "Noticed you posted about interest rate uncertainty in Nashville multifamily"
- "The Hendersonville deal you listed had some great unit-level renovation numbers"

## Research Shortcuts

When you don't have 30 minutes to research each prospect, use these fast methods:

### Method A: LinkedIn One-Page Scan (2 min/person)
1. Open their LinkedIn profile
2. Note: current title → deal type; company/fund → scale; recent activity tab → hook line
3. That's usually enough for a meaningful personalization

### Method B: Property Records Search (5 min/person)
1. Search county assessor site for properties owned by the person/entity
2. Most active operators have 3-8 recent acquisitions visible
3. Pick the most recent and use it as the hook

### Method C: Fund Website Quick Review (3 min/person)
1. Go to their fund's website
2. "Portfolio" page shows deal types and markets at a glance
3. "Team" or "About" page reveals scale and investment thesis
4. Pick one specific deal from their portfolio as the hook

### Method D: The "I Remember" Hook (0 min, highest trust)
When you genuinely remember something about them from your existing relationship:
- "You mentioned during lunch last month that underwriting was eating into your sourcing time..."
- "After our conversation at ULI about interest rates, I built something that might help..."
- This is the most powerful — if Andrey remembers a specific conversation, use it verbatim.

## Personalization Rules

1. **One specific detail is better than three generic ones.** Pick the most relevant fact and lead with it.
2. **The hook should feel like a natural opener, not a research flex.** "Saw your closing" works. "I noticed you purchased 3505 E Cesar Chavez St on March 14 via warranty deed" does not.
3. **Match the scale language.** Talk time savings to solo operators, capacity to syndicators, standardization to funds.
4. **Reference their actual market, not ours.** If they operate Nashville and we only have Austin data, acknowledge that: "even though I can't pull live Nashville comps right now, the underwriting framework works the same..."
5. **The relationship is the differentiator.** Every email should make them think "Andrey actually thought about MY situation" not "Andrey is sending this to everyone."
