2. **Negative cash flow at current leverage:** Unless seller financing or lower rate available, deal is cash-flow negative by ~$600K/year.

4. **Assessed values suggest upside:** Travis County assessed value of $29.6M matches asking price exactly — this is unusual. Typically, tax assessments lag market values. Either the property was recently reassessed, or the seller is using assessed value as anchor rather than market evidence.

### Low Priority Risks

8. **Travis County tax trends:** Property taxes in Travis County continue to rise, increasing annual carrying costs beyond stated NOI figures.

## 5. Recommended Next Steps

1. **Request full rent roll, service contracts, and T-12 financials.** Without these, any valuation is speculative. The gap between asking price ($29.6M) and the negative cash flow math means either: (a) the NOI is understated in marketing materials, or (b) the deal is overpriced for current market conditions.

2. **Get 3 current lender quotes.** At today's rates (check with local Austin credit unions and community banks), a 7.5% assumption may be conservative or aggressive — it determines whether this deal works at all.

3. **Verify TRD compliance status.** East Austin has specific Tenant Rights & Displacement regulations that could affect renovation plans, unit consolidation, or turnover strategy post-acquisition. This is material to the value-add thesis.

4. **Order Phase I environmental and physical inspection** before any LOI. These are non-negotiable cost items (~$3K–$5K combined).

5. **Bottom line:** At stated terms and current Austin multifamily market conditions, this deal does not cash-flow under conventional financing. The investment case requires either (a) a motivated seller accepting lower equity returns for liquidity, or (b) a significant and verified value-add plan (rent increases of 10%+, unit reconfiguration, amenity additions) with realistic absorption timelines given the local oversupply.
