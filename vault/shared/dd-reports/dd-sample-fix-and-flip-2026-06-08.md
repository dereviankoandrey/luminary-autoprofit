## 3. Risk Assessment

### High Priority Risks
1. **Zoning entitlement risk:** The current zoning says "SF-4" but does Travis County permit 4 separate lots, or is it a 4-unit duplex/quadplex? This distinction changes the entire project economics. Must confirm with Travis County Planning & Zoning before any commitment.
2. **Construction cost escalation:** At $275–$325/SF and rising material/labor costs in Austin, construction estimates have a ±15% variance band. If actuals run high, margins disappear quickly.
3. **Absorption risk:** If two townhomes don't sell within 90 days of completion, carrying costs eat into profit aggressively.

### Medium Priority Risks
4. **Existing structure condition:** "Significant deferred maintenance" on a 1978 building could reveal structural issues (foundation settlement, slab problems are common in Central Texas clay soil) that make the acquisition price less attractive even as land.
5. **Utility infrastructure:** Circle C Ranch has established utilities, but confirm sewer capacity for 4-unit development. The City of Austin's Site Plan Performance Metrics page shows average review cycle times — factor in a 60–90 day permit timeline.
6. **Interest rate sensitivity on construction loan:** If rates are above 8%, carrying costs increase substantially and may kill thin-margin projects.

### Low Priority Risks
7. **School district boundaries:** Travis County ISD boundaries affect buyer pool size. Verify the specific address is within the desired school catchment (Canyon Vista/Corrigan Middle).
8. **Flood zone status:** Check FEMA flood maps — if in a floodplain, insurance and build costs increase significantly.
