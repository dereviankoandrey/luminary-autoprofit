## Market Context: Washington D.C. / Arlington, VA

The DC-Arlington metro is the highest-cost entry market tested in this series, introducing fundamentally different deal dynamics than Sun Belt metros (Dallas, Phoenix, Charlotte). Key structural factors:

- **Median home price:** ~$575K–$625K (DC proper), $480K–$530K (Arlington County)
- **Property tax rate:** 1.02% DC / 0.85% Arlington — moderate, no insurance-risk premium
- **Absorption rate:** ~2.8 months inventory in Arlington (tighter than Sun Belt average of 4–5 months)
- **Days on market:** 18–24 days for well-priced single-family; 30+ for condos
- **Federal employment anchor:** 200K+ federal civilian workers + defense contractors create structural demand floor unlikely to correlate with housing cycles
- **Zoning constraints:** Arlington has undergone significant zoning reform (2026 Comprehensive Plan) allowing increased density near Metro stations — creates value-add opportunities in specific corridors

This market tests the engine under maximum price-complexity conditions. Deals that pencil here are structurally stronger than any prior market tested.

## Deal 1: Fix-and-Flip — Arlington County, Single-Family (2BR/1BA Bungalow)

**Assumptions:**
- ARV: $785,000 (comps in Glebe Rd corridor: $760K–$810K for renovated 3BR/1.5BA)
- Acquisition price: $620,000 (MLS asking; negotiated to ~$595K with 4% below-asking target)
- Reno budget: $125,000 (kitchen, baths, flooring, exterior refresh — cosmetic-heavy renovation)
- Holding costs: 6 months at $3,800/mo = $22,800 (hard money @ 10.5%)
- Closing costs: 4% buy + 2% sell = ~$47,800

**Hard Money Loan:** $530K | **Interest rate:** 10.5% | **Points:** 2

### Analysis

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total Acquisition Cost | $620,000 |
| Reno Budget | $125,000 |
| Holding Costs (6mo) | $22,800 |
| Closing Costs (buy+sell) | $47,800 |
| **Total Project Cost** | **$815,600** |
| ARV | $785,000 |
| **Net Profit** | **($30,600)** |
| ROI (on cash invested ~$95K) | **-32.2%** |

### Verdict: NO-GO at current pricing

**Why:** The DC-Arlington flip market is structurally unprofitable for cosmetic-heavy renovations at current price levels. The $785K ARV cap in this corridor leaves insufficient margin after acquisition, reno, and carrying costs.

**When it WOULD work:**
- Below-market acquisition: probate/pre-foreclosure at 15–20% below MLS ($496K–$527K range)
- Value-add renovation that adds a bedroom/bath (not just cosmetic): pushes ARV to $830K–$860K
- Faster hold period: 4 months instead of 6 saves ~$7,600 in holding costs

**Operator intelligence:** This is the second market after Phoenix where flip economics are channel-dependent. MLS flips at asking price do not work here. Off-market deals at significant discount are the only viable path — but off-market deal flow requires local relationships or paid data sources that this agent cannot autonomously access.

## Deal 2: Multifamily Value-Add — Prince George's County, MD (6-Unit)

**Assumptions:**
- Price: $1,450,000 | Units: 6 | Avg unit size: 750 sqft
- Current NOI: $98,000 (occupancy ~82%, rents below market by ~15%)
- Renovation budget: $120,000 ($20K/unit cosmetic upgrade — kitchen refresh, flooring, lighting)
- Post-renovation rents: +15% per unit → new NOI target: $114,000
- Cap rate: 5.8% (DC suburb premium for federal employment stability)
- Financing: 65% LTV CMBS at 7.25%, 30-year amort

### Analysis

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Acquisition Price | $1,450,000 |
| Reno Budget | $120,000 |
| Closing Costs (buy+reno) | ~$36,000 |
| **Total Project Cost** | **$1,606,000** |
| Post-Reno NOI | $114,000 |
| Post-Reno Value (@ 5.8% cap) | $1,965,500 |
| **Equity Gain** | **$359,500** |
| Cash-on-Cash (20% down = ~$290K) | **12.4%** |
| DSCR (post-reno) | 1.38x |

### Verdict: GO — HIGH CONVICTION with conditions

**Why it works:**
- Federal employment floor creates structural demand resilience even in downturns
- Rent gap of ~15% below market provides clear, quantifiable value-add path
- $20K/unit renovation is modest but sufficient to close the rent premium (cosmetic-heavy)
- 5.8% cap rate on stabilized value is reasonable for DC suburbs with government anchor

**Key risks:**
- CMBS financing in current rate environment may require higher equity (70–72% LTV instead of 65%)
- Prince George's County has lower appreciation trajectory than Arlington — expect ~3–4% annual, not 6–8%
- DSCR at 1.38x is adequate but leaves limited cushion for vacancy spikes

**Operator intelligence:** This deal type (small multifamily value-add near federal employment centers) is the most structurally sound opportunity in this market. The government anchor creates a demand floor that Sun Belt markets lack, making it recession-resilient even if appreciation is modest.

## Deal 3: Buy-and-Hold Duplex — Arlington County, VA (2-Unit)

**Assumptions:**
- Price: $895,000 | Units: 2 | Each unit ~900 sqft
- Unit A rent: $2,400/mo (owner occupies one unit — house-hack scenario)
- Unit B rent: $2,600/mo (rented out)
- Total gross income: $57,600/yr | Expenses (taxes, insurance, maintenance): ~$18,000/yr
- Net operating income: $39,600
- Financing: 25% down conventional ($223,750) at 7.0%, 30-year amort
- Monthly mortgage (P&I): ~$4,710 | Total monthly PITI + HOA est: ~$5,200

### Analysis

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Purchase Price | $895,000 |
| Down Payment (25%) | $223,750 |
| Closing Costs | ~$17,900 |
| **Total Cash Required** | **~$241,650** |
| Monthly Gross Rent | $5,000 |
| Monthly Expenses (est.) | ~$1,500 |
| Monthly Mortgage (P&I) | ~$4,710 |
| **Monthly Cash Flow** | **($1,210)** negative before reserves |

### Verdict: REVIEW — Negative cash flow at current rates

**Why it fails as pure investment:** At 7%+ interest rates and $895K pricing, a duplex in Arlington produces negative monthly cash flow even with the house-hack strategy. The math simply doesn't work for traditional buy-and-hold.

**When it WOULD work (house-hack scenario):**
- If Andrey occupies one unit: personal housing cost is effectively zero ($2,400/mo "saved")
- Net monthly position: -$1,210 cash flow + $2,400 imputed rent = **+$1,190/month net benefit**
- This converts a negative-cash-flow deal into a positive-living-experience deal
- Equity build (~$800/mo principal paydown) + appreciation (3–4% annually ≈ $27K/yr) adds ~$3,350/month in wealth accumulation

**Operator intelligence:** House-hacking Arlington duplexes is viable as a **wealth-building strategy**, not a cash-flow play. The federal employment proximity means the owner-occupant can walk to work, saving $1,500+/mo in commuting costs and time. Total economic benefit (cash flow deficit + imputed rent + principal paydown + appreciation) is positive when you count the lifestyle premium of Arlington living near Metro.
