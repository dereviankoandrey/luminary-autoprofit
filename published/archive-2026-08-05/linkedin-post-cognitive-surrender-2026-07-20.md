## Post Text

A new study from researchers at École Normale Supérieure and Sapienza University of Rome found something that should terrify anyone making financial decisions with AI.

When people had access to AI advice:
- Their accuracy dropped from 27% to 9%. Three times worse.
- Their willingness to say "I don't know" collapsed from 44% to 3%.
- Their confidence rose from 30% to 76%. More than double.

They didn't just get wrong answers. They became worse at recognizing they were wrong. Wharton researchers call this "cognitive surrender." The AI doesn't just give bad advice — it degrades the person's ability to question it.

I've spent 20 years as a real estate developer and CFO managing $4B+ in portfolio value. Every deal I've ever closed came down to one thing: can you trace every number back to its source?

Black-box AI models are now telling investors whether to buy properties worth millions. They don't show their work. They don't flag assumptions. They give confident answers about deals that may not pencil at all.

That's why I built a deterministic underwriting engine that shows exactly how it arrives at every decision — cap rates, NOI projections, exit valuations, risk flags. Every number is traceable. Every assumption is stated. If the math doesn't work, you see why. Not from a model that got lucky once. From an engine that's been stress-tested across 11+ deal scenarios and can't be wrong for the same reason twice.

Because in real estate, confidence without verification isn't an advantage. It's how you lose money on deals that look good until they don't.
