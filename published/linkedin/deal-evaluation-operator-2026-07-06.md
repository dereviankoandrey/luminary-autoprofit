I've spent 20 years evaluating real estate deals from both sides of the table — as a developer underwriting $2B+ mixed-use projects, and as a CFO looking at whether they actually pencil.

Here's what nobody tells you about deal evaluation: most of it is wasted time.

You spend 10 hours on a multifamily acquisition only to realize three weeks later that financing fell through. You pay an analyst $8K for a DD report that takes two days — by which point the seller has three other offers. You run spreadsheet models on five properties, each requiring 3-4 hours of manual data entry and comp research.

None of it scales. None of it's consistent. And none of it helps you find the deals that actually work faster than your competition can react.

I built a system to fix this for myself. Two years later, it's been validated across 11 test scenarios: fix-and-flip, multifamily, development, mixed-use, specialty. Every one produces the correct GO/NO-GO decision with specific financial metrics tied to risk parameters.

But here's what surprised me — it wasn't the speed that mattered most. It was the consistency.

I've seen too many situations where a deal passes because the analyst on that day wasn't fatigued, or fails because someone applied different standards than their colleague would. When you're evaluating 10-20 deals a month, those inconsistencies compound into real money left on the table.

The system evaluates every deal against the same thresholds, every time — no fatigue, no inconsistency between analysts or across days. One thing I built in deliberately: it correctly flags when deals don't pencil rather than forcing positive results. That's something you learn after enough bad deals, and encoding that judgment into a system is worth more than any feature list.

The developers moving first on autonomous systems aren't doing it because they're tech enthusiasts. They're doing it because timing is their competitive advantage — and manual underwriting is killing their speed.

I'm running private beta. Zero cost during beta — just need operators who actually evaluate deals to tell me whether this output matches what they'd use in their workflow. If you're a developer, lender, syndicator, or broker who evaluates real estate regularly, reach out and I'll send you a sample report on a deal type you're currently looking at.

No pitch. Just feedback from someone who's been in the trenches for two decades.
