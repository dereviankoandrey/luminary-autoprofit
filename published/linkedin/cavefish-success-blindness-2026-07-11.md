# Cavefish Principle — Success Blindness (v1)

**Source:** approved/linkedin_post_successful_companies_go_blind_2026-07-11.md  
**Formatted:** 2026-08-03  
**Platform:** LinkedIn short-form post  

---

There's a creature called the Mexican cavefish that lost its eyes over a million years ago.

But here's what scientists found: even after its eyes disappeared, it kept the genes for building them. The genetic code for sight survived long after the trait itself was gone.

I think something similar happens to companies once they achieve success.

The real estate underwriting industry is a perfect example.

For decades, developers have made billion-dollar decisions on spreadsheet models and gut instinct. It worked. These guys got rich. They built portfolios. They became "successful."

But the world changed while they were busy being right.

Construction costs doubled in some markets. Interest rates went from near-zero to 8%. Remote work reshaped commercial real estate overnight. New financing structures emerged that traditional underwriters don't know how to model.

Yet many of these same operators are still using the same spreadsheet templates they built in 2015 — because those templates "worked" for twenty years and nobody wants to admit their methodology is obsolete.

The cavefish kept its eye genes after losing its eyes. These companies keep their old underwriting spreadsheets after the market moved on.

I've been building deterministic financial analysis tools because I saw this blind spot firsthand. After 20 years in real estate development — from $4B mixed-use projects to fix-and-flip deals — I realized the gap wasn't that we didn't have data. It was that we were using the wrong framework to process it.

Most AI underwriting tools solve the wrong problem. They make analysis faster but don't make it more accurate. That's like giving a cavefish a better pair of eyes when what it actually needs is to realize its eyes are gone.

We built something different: an engine that doesn't just crunch numbers faster, but applies auditable methodology to financial decisions that used to be made on faith and spreadsheets from 2015.

The difference between "faster wrong answers" and "correct answers you can verify line-by-line" is the difference between building wealth and losing it.

If you're running deals in today's market with yesterday's methodology, I'd love to show you what actually underwriting looks like when the math doesn't change its mind.
