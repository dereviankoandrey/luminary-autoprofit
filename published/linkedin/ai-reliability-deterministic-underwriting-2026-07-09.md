I've been reading a lot of developers talk about "LLM burnout" lately.

Not the kind where you're tired from working too hard. The kind where you're exhausted because every AI tool you use keeps sounding confident while being wrong.

False assumptions presented as facts. Staccato fragments of advice that sound good but don't hold up under scrutiny. Hallucinated numbers dressed up as analysis.

I've spent 20 years in real estate development and finance. I've sat through board meetings where decisions were made on spreadsheet models that turned out to be wrong because someone assumed wrong about vacancy rates or construction costs.

The problem isn't AI. The problem is using general-purpose AI for specialized financial work.

When you ask ChatGPT to "analyze this deal," it gives you a plausible-sounding summary. It doesn't calculate IRR. It doesn't stress-test DSCR against rate changes. It doesn't flag that the rent assumptions exceed comparable properties by 15%.

It sounds right. That's the danger.

So we built something different — not another AI wrapper, but a deterministic underwriting engine for real estate. It runs on the same principle as any engineering system: measurable inputs, auditable methodology, correct outputs every time.

Not "AI-assisted" analysis. Deterministic financial analysis with AI handling the workflow.

The difference matters when you're putting your own capital on the line or advising someone who is.

If you're tired of AI tools that sound confident but can't be trusted with numbers — I'd love to show you what deterministic actually looks like in practice.
