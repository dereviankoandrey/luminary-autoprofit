## Product Thesis

1Mind proved that B2B buyers will pay enterprise prices for Superhuman GTM agents when the agent can qualify, educate, book, support deals, and compress sales cycles. Augur's opportunity is not to outspend 1Mind on cloud avatars. It is to package a private, owned Superhuman GTM agent under Ultr8/LBV for customers who want the business outcome without handing their GTM brain and customer memory to a closed enterprise SaaS platform.

The wedge: private GTM Brain, faster consulting-led launch, lower starting price, customer-specific knowledge isolation, and optional face as an upgrade rather than a dependency.

## Initial Offer

Name: Yahli for Serendipity

Positioning:
- A private AI GTM agent trained on Serendipity's offer, pitch, proof, objections, and buyer conversations.
- Starts as voice/waveform on the website.
- Qualifies inbound buyers.
- Answers from approved knowledge.
- Books Calendly calls.
- Learns from interactions through Augur's owned context graph.
- Adds face later through Tavus or HeyGen only if it improves conversion or premium perception.

Primary buyer promise:
- "Turn your best GTM explanation into an always-on voice agent that qualifies buyers and books calls, while you keep control of the brain, data, and guardrails."

Do not say:
- "Fully autonomous closer."
- "Guaranteed revenue lift."
- "Human replacement."
- "1Mind clone."

## Market Position

### Against 1Mind

1Mind:
- Enterprise and mid-market B2B.
- Reported $100K-$400K ACV in the Latka interview.
- Cloud platform.
- Full Superhuman category leadership.
- Face, voice, GTM Brain, multiple surfaces.

Augur:
- Private/owned implementation.
- Consulting-led first, productized second.
- Voice/waveform first, face optional.
- Lower entry price.
- Repeatable onboarding and ingestion pipeline as the moat.
- Built around customer-specific KB isolation and deterministic guardrails.

Recommended competitive line:
"1Mind is the premium enterprise category leader. Augur is the private/owned implementation path for founders and operators who want Superhuman GTM capacity without a six-figure cloud-only enterprise commitment."

### Against Generic Chatbots

Generic chatbot:
- Text-first.
- Weak memory.
- Weak workflow accountability.
- Often top-of-funnel only.

Augur:
- Voice-first.
- Context graph memory.
- Tool-aware booking and CRM handoff.
- Guardrails and source-aware answers.
- Built around bottom-of-funnel outcomes: qualified meetings, faster buyer education, revived opportunities.

### Against Avatar Tools

Avatar tools:
- Sell the face.
- Often hosted and credit-metered.
- May not own the GTM workflow.

Augur:
- Sells the GTM Brain and the implementation.
- Buys face only when useful.
- Keeps voice/waveform as the default lower-friction tier.

## Packaging Path

### Package 1: Yahli Voice MVP

For Serendipity and first reference customers.

Includes:
- Single-tenant voice/waveform web widget.
- Serendipity KB ingestion.
- Yahli persona and guardrails.
- Calendly booking flow.
- Session summaries.
- Latency and quality report.

Purpose:
- Prove single-tenant quality and booking conversion.
- Generate demos and internal case study.

Suggested price wedge:
- Setup: $5K-$15K.
- Monthly: $1K-$3K.
- Usage: fair-use voice minutes or pass-through above threshold.

Rationale:
- Far below 1Mind's reported $100K-$400K enterprise range.
- High enough to avoid commodity chatbot positioning.
- Lets Augur sell implementation and owned brain before face is ready.

### Package 2: Private GTM Brain

For customers with enough content, CRM context, and inbound flow.

Includes:
- Everything in Voice MVP.
- Context graph over offer, pitch deck, CRM read adapter, conversation memory.
- Source mapping and freshness metadata.
- Deterministic tool policies.
- Qualification schema.
- CRM note draft/export.
- Red-team guardrail pack.

Suggested price wedge:
- Setup: $15K-$35K.
- Monthly: $3K-$7.5K.
- Add usage/conversation tiering only after benchmark economics are known.

Purpose:
- The first real product, not just demo.
- Builds the durable moat: ingestion, context graph, guardrails, workflow routing.

### Package 3: Superhuman Face Tier

For customers who need premium presence.

Includes:
- Tavus face tier primary.
- HeyGen LiveAvatar fallback.
- Persona/voice/brand matching.
- Measured credit economics.
- Same Augur GTM Brain, not vendor-hosted brain by default.

Suggested price wedge:
- Setup add-on: $5K-$20K depending on avatar creation effort.
- Monthly add-on: vendor cost plus margin, or premium tier.
- Usage: pass-through with managed cap until economics are proven.

Purpose:
- Premium upsell after voice/waveform is useful.
- Avoid face-first complexity.

### Package 4: Ultr8/LBV White-Label GTM Agent

For repeatable productization after Serendipity validates.

Includes:
- Per-customer KB isolation.
- Onboarding/ingestion pipeline.
- Persona/voice/branding.
- Web widget.
- Calendly and CRM connectors.
- Optional face.
- Admin review dashboard.
- Analytics and monthly optimization.

Suggested price wedge:
- Starter: $10K setup + $2K/month.
- Growth: $25K setup + $5K/month.
- Premium Face: $40K+ setup + $8K-$15K/month plus usage.

The exact numbers should be validated against labor, usage costs, and buyer willingness. The important strategy is to sit below 1Mind enterprise ACV while staying above chatbot/app-builder pricing.

## Moat

The moat is not the avatar.

The moat is:
- Repeatable knowledge ingestion.
- Persona engineering tied to a real offer.
- Context graph memory across interactions.
- Deterministic action orchestration.
- Guardrail/red-team pack.
- Per-customer isolation.
- Conversion analytics.
- Implementation speed.
- Operator trust that data and GTM logic remain owned/private.

Every delivery should produce reusable assets:
- Source map.
- Objection map.
- Persona policy.
- Tool schema.
- Guardrail tests.
- Latency report.
- Conversion report.
- Onboarding checklist updates.

## First Demo Script

Use Yahli on a Serendipity page.

Flow:
- Visitor asks what Serendipity does.
- Yahli gives a concise answer from the KB.
- Visitor states a real pain: inbound demand, poor follow-up, unclear GTM messaging.
- Yahli asks two qualification questions.
- Yahli explains relevant Serendipity path without overclaiming.
- Visitor asks about results or guarantees.
- Yahli declines guarantees and reframes around diagnostic call.
- Yahli offers Calendly booking.
- Visitor books or receives the link.
- Internal view shows transcript, qualification summary, source references, tool events, latency.

## Pricing Strategy

Use 1Mind as the price umbrella, not as the direct first price.

- 1Mind's reported $100K-$400K ACV proves enterprise budget exists.
- Augur should enter below that with a consulting/product hybrid.
- Avoid $99/month SaaS framing; it kills trust and cannot fund implementation.
- Use setup fee for ingestion, persona, guardrails, and integration.
- Use monthly fee for hosting, optimization, support, and analytics.
- Use usage caps to avoid uncontrolled voice/video costs.
- Charge face separately until Tavus/HeyGen economics are known.

Recommended first customer pricing:
- Serendipity internal/reference build: cost-accounted, not public pricing.
- First paid pilot: $7.5K-$15K setup + $1.5K-$3K/month.
- First GTM Brain customer: $20K-$35K setup + $3K-$7.5K/month.
- Face tier: add only after provider validation.

## Risks

- Latency risk: local STT/LLM/TTS may not feel natural on current cluster hardware. Benchmark before claims.
- Voice quality risk: local TTS may be less premium than cloud voices. Keep local as default for privacy; test quality fallbacks later.
- Vendor risk: Tavus/HeyGen credit models and concurrency may change. Keep face optional.
- Control risk: Steno may be too hosted for the private/owned promise.
- Scope risk: CRM writes and live-call ride-along can expand complexity too early.
- Trust risk: AI identity and overclaims must be tightly guarded.

## Immediate Recommendation

Build the voice/waveform Yahli MVP first. Sell the product as private GTM capacity, not an avatar. Use the first Serendipity implementation to produce the proof pack: demo, benchmark, source map, guardrail tests, booking flow, and conversion metrics. Only then decide whether Tavus face materially improves close rate or premium packaging.
